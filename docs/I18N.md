# i18n keyword and terminology research: AI robots.txt tool site

Research date: 2026-11-04. Locales: de, fr, es, it, nl, pl, pt-BR, ja.

## How this was researched, and its limits

- **Method.** About 60 WebSearch queries in each language. They covered how-to phrasing ("block GPTBot", "robots.txt tester", ".htaccess/nginx"), local SEO blogs, hosting help pages, news sites and legal commentary. Search results arrive as summaries plus URLs.
- **WebFetch was mostly blocked.** In this sandbox it failed with ENOTFOUND for non-allowlisted hosts (cuatrecasas.com, standardyprawa.pl), so primary statute texts could not be opened.
- **Search budget ran out** (it is shared across agents) before the last Japanese queries ("robots.txt 書き方", AI-bot server rules in JA). Those points rely on prior domain knowledge and are marked **[unverified]**.
- **How legal statements are tagged:**
  - **[verified]**: confirmed by at least one search result or snippet.
  - **[from memory – verify]**: needs a check against the official text before publishing (Gesetze-im-Internet, Légifrance, BOE, Normattiva, wetten.overheid.nl, ISAP, Planalto, e-Gov).
- **Title lengths.** Character counts for titles were computed. Japanese titles target about 28–40 full-width characters, because Japanese SERPs truncate by pixel width, so 50–60 JA characters would be far too long.

## Cross-locale context (applies to every language)

1. **New shared AI-training opt-out token (major, 2026-10-30) [verified via multiple outlets]**
   - The token is `__usage_ai-training`. It is used as a User-Agent group in robots.txt, for example `User-Agent: __usage_ai-training` / `Disallow: /` / `Allow: /press-kit/`.
   - Source draft: `draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal-00`, written by Kevin Kelley (Anthropic) and based on the IETF AIPREF vocabulary draft. It is not an IETF standard yet.
   - Reported vendor plans:
     - Anthropic has started supporting it.
     - Microsoft starts from 2027-02-01 and will also offer separate controls for training and grounding.
     - Google plans support in early 2027.
     - OpenAI says "coming months".
     - Meta has not committed.
   - The `User-agent: *` wildcard does **not** count as a training preference.
   - Local coverage already exists: ITmedia (JA, 2026-11-01) and Punto Informatico (IT). Each locale should have a short explainer or FAQ about this; it is a strong fresh-content opportunity.
2. **Cloudflare**
   - Cloudflare has blocked AI crawlers by default for new domains since July 2025.
   - In Sept 2026 it added a setting that disallows AI training while keeping search crawling (ITmedia: 「AI学習の不許可」; heise and Emerce also covered it). Google, Apple and Microsoft multi-purpose crawlers honour this setting.
   - Every locale's SEO press warns that a CDN or WAF can block bots that robots.txt allows. This makes a good "tester" feature hook.
3. **Google retired the Search Console robots.txt tester in Dec 2023 [verified, cited by DE/PL/JA tool pages].** Every locale's tester pages use this as their reason to exist, so mention it in the tester intro.
4. **EU layer for de, fr, es, it, nl, pl:**
   - AI Act Art. 53(1)(c) requires general-purpose AI (GPAI) providers to have a copyright policy that identifies and complies with DSM Art. 4(3) reservations, "including through state-of-the-art technologies". Recital 105 makes the same point.
   - The GPAI Code of Practice (Copyright chapter, July 2025) commits signatories to follow robots.txt per RFC 9309 **[from memory – verify wording]**.
   - Use each country's own name for the Act:
     - DE: KI-Verordnung (KI-VO)
     - FR: règlement sur l'IA / RIA / "AI Act"
     - ES: Reglamento de IA (RIA)
     - IT: AI Act / Regolamento sull'IA
     - NL: AI-verordening
     - PL: AI Act / akt w sprawie sztucznej inteligencji
   - Local names for the DSM Directive: DE "DSM-Richtlinie", FR "directive DAMUN", NL "DSM-richtlijn", PL "dyrektywa DSM", IT "direttiva Copyright".
5. **Common framing in every locale: "training bots vs. search/citation bots vs. user-fetch bots".**
   - Every market's SEO press uses this framing: SISTRIX, WebRankInfo, Conversion, Frankwatching, 海外SEO情報ブログ and others.
   - The "block training, stay visible in AI search" guide (f) is the highest-intent guide everywhere.
6. **Proposed English base slugs (for parity).** Recommendation: translate slugs for de/fr/es/it/nl/pl/pt-br and keep English for ja.
   - Tools: `/robots-txt-tester/`, `/bots/` (bot page: `/bots/gptbot/`), `/server-rules/`, `/llms-txt-generator/`, `/content-signals/`, `/tdmrep/`
   - Guides: `/guides/block-ai-training-keep-ai-search/`, `/guides/robots-txt-syntax/`, `/guides/eu-ai-act-tdm-opt-out/`, `/guides/robots-txt-bad-bots/`
   - Legal and info pages: `/privacy/`, `/terms/`, `/about/`, `/licenses/`
   - Keep `content-signals` and `tdmrep` untranslated in **all** locales. They are proper names (Cloudflare Content Signals Policy; W3C TDM Reservation Protocol).
   - Use `pt-br` (lowercase) as the URL segment and `pt-BR` in hreflang.

## German (de)

### 1. Terminology
- **"KI" is the standard term**, used by heise, t3n, SISTRIX, onlinemarketing.de, seo-suedwest, Wix DE and the legal press. "AI" appears only in brand or product names (AI Overviews, AI Act) and in machine-translated tool pages.
- **Crawlers:**
  - "**KI-Crawler**" is the most common term (Wix DE: "KI-Crawler auf deiner Website blockieren"; gradually.ai; SISTRIX).
  - "**KI-Bots**" is also common (heise "Cloudflare vs. KI-Bots", seo-suedwest).
  - Related terms: "Trainings-Bots / Trainings-Crawler", "KI-Suche", "Grounding-Bots" (seokratie), "**KI-Opt-out**" (SISTRIX article title).
- **Verbs:** "blockieren" dominates; "sperren" and "aussperren" are colloquial. For the tester: "robots.txt **prüfen** / **testen**", and "robots.txt Tester" is used verbatim.
- **Spelling:** Duden-correct compounds are "robots.txt-Generator", "robots.txt-Datei", "llms.txt-Generator" and "KI-Crawler". Many tools write "robots.txt Generator" without the hyphen; both forms are fine in titles. Use „…" quotation marks.

### 4. Local nuance
- **Statute [verified]: § 44b UrhG (Text und Data Mining).**
  - Abs. 3: uses are permitted only if the rightsholder has not reserved them. "Ein Nutzungsvorbehalt bei online zugänglichen Werken ist nur dann wirksam, wenn er in **maschinenlesbarer Form** erfolgt."
  - Use the local key terms **"Nutzungsvorbehalt"** and **"maschinenlesbar"**.
  - § 60d UrhG is the research TDM exception (DSM Art. 3).
  - Implemented by the Gesetz zur Anpassung des Urheberrechts an die Erfordernisse des digitalen Binnenmarktes, in force 7 June 2021 **[from memory – verify]**.
- **Case law: Kneschke v. LAION.**
  - LG Hamburg, 27.9.2024, 310 O 227/23 **[verified]**: the claim was dismissed under § 60d. The court indicated (obiter) that a natural-language reservation in the terms of service could count as "machine-readable".
  - OLG Hamburg, 10.12.2025, 5 U 104/24 **[verified via commentary]**: the appeal court found the photo agency's reservation was not in the required machine-readable form. Commentary reads the ruling as making robots.txt or metadata the practical route.
  - **This is the key hook for the German EU guide.**
- **GEMA v. OpenAI (LG München I, Nov 2025):** song lyrics memorised in the model were held to infringe **[from memory – verify]**. Useful context, but it is about output and memorisation rather than robots.txt.
- **SISTRIX "KI-Opt-out" article** ranks for the topic and uses the "Training vs. Grounding" split. heise reported (Sept 2026) that Bing has no robots.txt training-only opt-out yet, and Microsoft's comes in early 2027.
- **German site obligations:**
  - An **Impressum** is required under § 5 DDG (the Digitale-Dienste-Gesetz replaced the TMG in May 2024) **[from memory – verify]**.
  - A **Datenschutzerklärung** is expected.
  - Plan both pages.

### 4. Local nuance
- **Statute [verified]:** **CPI art. L. 122-5-3** (fouille de textes et de données).
  - Paragraph III gives the general TDM exception, which applies unless the author "s'y est opposé de manière appropriée, notamment par des procédés lisibles par machine" for online content.
  - Art. **R. 122-28**: "L'opposition … n'a pas à être motivée et peut être exprimée **par tout moyen**".
  - Databases: L. 342-3.
  - Transposed by Ordonnance n° 2021-1518 (24 Nov 2021); décret n° 2022-928 (23 June 2022) **[verify numbers]**.
- **Local legal terms:** "**fouille de textes et de données**" (official), "TDM", "**droit d'opposition / opposition**", "opt-out", "réservation des droits". "Moissonnage" is used for web scraping (CNIL and Village de la Justice use it).
- **Pending bill [verified]:** PPL Darcos, "présomption d'exploitation des contenus culturels par les fournisseurs d'IA".
  - Creates a rebuttable presumption via a new CPI art. L. 331-4-1.
  - The Sénat adopted it unanimously on 8 Apr 2026.
  - At the Assemblée nationale (n° 2634), the culture committee adopted it around June 2026 (report n° 2864). Final adoption is **not confirmed** as of this research.
- **TDMRep has French roots** (EDRLab, the French publishing sector). The ADAGP opt-out guide cites robots.txt, ai.txt and tdmrep.json. Give the `/fr/tdmrep/` page priority.
- **CNIL:** guidance on web scraping (moissonnage) for AI training recommends respecting robots.txt-style opposition **[from memory – verify]**.
- **French site obligations:** "**mentions légales**" are mandatory under the LCEN (Loi pour la confiance dans l'économie numérique), art. 6 **[from memory – verify]**.

### 4. Local nuance
- **Statute [verified]:** the TDM provisions are **not** in the TRLPI.
  - Spain implemented both DSM exceptions (Art. 3 and Art. 4) in a **single article 67 of Real Decreto-ley 24/2021** (2 Nov 2021), a standalone norm.
  - **Art. 67.3**: the general TDM permission does not apply where the rightsholder "**ha reservado expresamente**" the use.
  - Commentary notes that Spain did not copy the Directive's "por medios de lectura mecánica" wording literally **[verify exact text, BOE-A-2021-17910]**.
  - A bill to reform RDL 24/2021 lapsed when the Cortes were dissolved (FESABID).
- **Local legal terms:** "**minería de textos y datos**", "**reserva de derechos**", "reservado expresamente", "lectura mecánica" (the Directive's Spanish text), "opt-out".
- **Latin America:** none of these countries has an EU-style TDM opt-out (Chile and others are debating one). Keep the guide EU and Spain-specific and add a short note for Latin American readers.
- **Spain's AI authority** is AESIA. A draft "Ley para el buen uso y la gobernanza de la IA" (2025) exists **[from memory – verify status]**.
- **Spanish site obligations:** an "**aviso legal**" is required under LSSI-CE art. 10 **[from memory – verify]**.
